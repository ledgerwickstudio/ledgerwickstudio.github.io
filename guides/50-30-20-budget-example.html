The 50/30/20 Budget, Explained With a Real Example

LedgerwickStudio · Updated 2026-10-09

The 50/30/20 budget splits your take-home pay (after taxes) into three buckets. It's popular because it takes five minutes to set up and doesn't ask you to plan 40 categories.

  • 50% needs: what you must pay to live and work — rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport, minimum debt payments.
  • 30% wants: everything optional — dining out, subscriptions, travel, hobbies, upgrades.
  • 20% savings and debt: emergency fund, retirement, goals and any payment above the minimum on debts.

Example: $4,000 take-home per month

BucketShareMonthly targetExample
Needs50%$2,000Rent $1,300 · groceries $380 · utilities $160 · transport $160
Wants30%$1,200Dining $300 · subscriptions $60 · travel fund $400 · other $440
Savings & debt20%$800Emergency fund $400 · retirement $250 · extra debt $150

Three common mistakes

  • Using gross pay. The split is on what actually reaches your account.
  • Hiding wants inside needs. Groceries are a need; takeout is a want.
  • Never checking. A budget only works if you compare the plan with what you really spent, at least once a month.

When to change the split

If rent alone takes 45% of your income, 50/30/20 won't fit — and that's fine. Start with your real numbers, then move a few points at a time from wants to savings. The split is a target, not a rule.

Paid every two weeks? Budgeting by paycheck instead of by month avoids the classic "rent is due before payday" problem.
