Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche: A Worked Example

LedgerwickStudio · Updated 2026-10-16

Both methods use the same rule: pay the minimum on every debt, and put every extra dollar on one target debt. When the target is paid off, its payment rolls over to the next one. The only difference is the order.

  • Snowball: target the smallest balance first. You close accounts sooner, which keeps many people motivated.
  • Avalanche: target the highest interest rate first. It usually saves the most interest.

The example

Four debts, $445 of total minimum payments, plus $250 extra each month ($695 in total):

DebtBalanceAPRMinimum
Store card$85026.49%$30
Credit card$4,20022.99%$105
Personal loan$2,40011.99%$80
Car loan$7,6006.90%$230

The results

MethodPayoff order (month paid off)Debt-free inTotal interest
SnowballStore card (4) → Personal loan (10) → Credit card (19) → Car loan (25)25 months$2,062.85
AvalancheStore card (4) → Credit card (16) → Personal loan (19) → Car loan (25)25 months$1,871.62
No extra $250Snowball order, rolling payments over45 months$4,616.74

Simplified simulation: interest is charged monthly at APR ÷ 12, payments stay fixed, no new charges or fees. Real loans compute interest slightly differently.

What the numbers say

  • Here the avalanche saves $191.23 in interest; the debt-free date is the same.
  • The snowball closes a second account 6 months earlier (month 10 vs. month 16). If quick wins keep you going, that can be worth more than $191.
  • The biggest lever is not the method — it's the extra payment: $250 a month cuts 20 months and about $2,550 of interest.
